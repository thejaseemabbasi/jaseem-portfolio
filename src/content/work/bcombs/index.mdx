Flagship cultural platforms carry strict visibility, localization, and launch-date requirements. At Technyx Systems, I led delivery across the [Zayed National Museum](https://zayednationalmuseum.ae/en/) digital ecosystem—spanning a seven-language Sitecore CMS website alongside companion iOS and Android mobile applications.

## The challenge

Delivering a public-facing national museum platform across seven languages meant coordinating parallel web, mobile, CMS, content, and QA tracks without letting localization bottlenecks slip the launch window.

- Multi-language content workflows required tight sequencing between engineering, content specialists, and QA.
- Website and mobile app releases needed unified milestone tracking and dependency management.
- Executive stakeholders required predictable reporting on scope, budget, and launch readiness.

## What I did

I established a structured delivery cadence that aligned web, mobile, and content tracks under one unified schedule—breaking down dependencies early so localization, QA, and deployment could run in controlled sprints rather than creating a late-stage bottleneck.

Daily cross-channel triage and clear milestone ownership kept engineering and client teams aligned on priorities, change requests, and release readiness from scoping through launch.

## Key decisions

**Sequence content and engineering in parallel.** Instead of treating seven-language localization as a final step, content workflows and QA batches were planned alongside core platform milestones.

**Single source of truth for scope.** Clear work breakdown structures and acceptance criteria prevented late-stage scope drift across web and mobile streams.

**Proactive risk tracking.** Bottlenecks in localization and cross-platform QA were surfaced early enough to rebalance team allocation without jeopardizing the target launch.
